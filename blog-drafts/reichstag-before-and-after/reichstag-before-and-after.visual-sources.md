# Visual sources — reichstag-before-and-after

Five images forming a before/after sequence: today's building, then the
imperial original, the 1933 fire, the 1945 ruin, and the modern dome inside.
Masters optimized to 1600px long edge, JPEG q86, uploaded to Wix Media. Raw
originals kept local only under `images/raw/` (gitignored); rebuild from the
source URLs below.

Attribution is required for the cover (CC BY-SA 3.0 DE). The three public-domain
files and the one CC0 file need no attribution but are credited anyway in the
article's native "Image credits" disclosure for consistency (one entry per
image = 5).

The famous Yevgeny Khaldei "Raising a Flag over the Reichstag" (1945) photo was
deliberately NOT used: its international copyright status is disputed. The
British Army ruin photo below (Imperial War Museum, public domain) carries the
same "after the battle" beat safely. A photo of the 1995 Christo & Jeanne-Claude
"Wrapped Reichstag" was also avoided: the wrapping was a temporary artwork, so
German freedom of panorama does not apply and such photos are a licensing trap.
The wrapping is covered in the article text and the widget instead.

## 1. Cover — `cover-reichstag-sunset.jpg`
- Subject: The modern Reichstag at sunset from the west, warm-lit stone facade,
  glass dome, German flag, TV Tower on the horizon.
- Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Reichstagsgeb%C3%A4ude_bei_Sonnenuntergang,_170325,_ako.jpg
- Author: Ansgar Koreng
- Licence: CC BY-SA 3.0 DE — https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/de/deed.en
- Why: Instantly readable "Reichstag today" image, strong at card-crop size.
  Editorial-grade cover and the "after" bookend of the sequence.

## 2. `reichstag-1900-imperial-dome.jpg`
- Subject: The Reichstag around 1900 with its original stone-and-glass dome and
  the Bismarck monument in front, on the imperial Königsplatz.
- Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Berlin_Reichstag_mit_Bismarck_Denkmal_um_1900.jpg
- Author: Unknown (period photograph)
- Licence: Public domain — https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/
- Why: The imperial "before" state, showing the first dome that the fire later
  destroyed.

## 3. `reichstag-fire-1933.jpg`
- Subject: The Reichstag burning on the night of 27 February 1933, flames behind
  the "Dem Deutschen Volke" facade and the silhouetted dome.
- Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Reichstagsbrand.jpg
- Author: Unknown (period photograph)
- Licence: Public domain — https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/
- Why: The turning point of the whole story, and a genuinely historic image.

## 4. `reichstag-ruins-1945.jpg`
- Subject: The bombed-out Reichstag on 3 June 1945 after the Battle of Berlin,
  the "Dem Deutschen Volke" inscription still legible on the scarred facade,
  rubble in the foreground.
- Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ruins_of_the_Reichstag_in_Berlin,_3_June_1945._BU8573.jpg
- Author: No 5 Army Film & Photographic Unit (Sgt Hewitt)
- Licence: Public domain (Imperial War Museum) — https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/mark/1.0/
- Why: The "after the war" ruin, the low point before the rebuild.

## 5. `reichstag-dome-interior.jpg`
- Subject: Inside Norman Foster's glass dome — the mirrored central cone, the
  spiral ramp and visitors above the debating chamber.
- Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Interior_of_the_Reichstag_dome_2015_2.jpg
- Author: Perituss
- Licence: CC0 1.0 — https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/
- Why: The modern democratic layer you can actually walk into, closing the arc.

## Rejected
- `File:German Reichstag 1898 (in Russian).jpg`: turned out to be an election
  map of the Empire, not the building.
- `File:Raising a flag over the Reichstag - Restoration.jpg` (Khaldei, 1945):
  iconic but internationally disputed copyright — avoided.
- `File:Dem Deutschen Volke - Flickr - abbilder (1).jpg`: a nice wide dusk view
  but redundant with the sunset cover.
