People land at Alexanderplatz, see the concrete, the trams, the fast food and the crowds, and quietly panic that they have booked the wrong part of Berlin. I understand the reaction. Alexanderplatz is not pretty. But it is the single best base for a first walk in the city, because almost everything a first-time visitor wants to see is within a short walk of it, and you do not need a ticket, a plan, or a single euro of transport to reach them.

This is a guide to what you can actually reach on foot from the World Clock at Alexanderplatz, sorted by how far you have to walk. If you are staying near here, or your tour meets here, you can see the historic core of Berlin in an afternoon without touching the U-Bahn.

## Why Alexanderplatz works as a base

Alexanderplatz sits at the edge of the historic centre of the former East Berlin. Walk west from it and you move through the oldest and most visited part of the city in a more or less straight line. The square itself is a transport hub, so you can arrive from anywhere and leave from anywhere, and the walk out is downhill in the sense that it only gets prettier.

## Within a five minute walk

Right around the square, before you have really started:

- **The World Clock (Weltzeituhr):** the meeting point for half the city and the spot where my tour starts. It tells the time in cities around the world and has done since 1969.
- **The TV Tower (Fernsehturm):** the tall one you cannot miss. You can go up for a paid view, but you can also just stand under it, which is free and more impressive than people expect.
- **St. Mary's Church (Marienkirche):** one of the oldest buildings in Berlin, quietly medieval, right next to all the concrete.
- **The Rotes Rathaus:** the red-brick city hall, two minutes west.

## Within a fifteen minute walk

This is where the classic Berlin postcard starts:

- **Museum Island:** five major museums on one island in the Spree, plus the [Berliner Dom](https://www.berlinwalk.com/post/is-museum-island-free-tickets-prices-and-what-to-actually-skip). Even if you skip the museums, the island itself is worth the walk.
- **The Spree riverbank:** the water that built Berlin, with boat tours leaving from the bridges.
- **The Nikolaiviertel:** the reconstructed old town, small and a bit artificial, but a pleasant detour.

## Within a twenty five minute walk

Keep going west and you reach the sights most people think of first:

- **Unter den Linden:** the grand boulevard running toward the Brandenburg Gate.
- **The Brandenburg Gate:** about 25 to 30 minutes on foot, and the walk there passes most of the centre. Worth knowing: the Gate stood in **East Berlin**, which surprises almost everyone.
- **The Reichstag dome:** free to visit but you must register in advance, which I explain in the [Reichstag dome guide](https://www.berlinwalk.com/post/how-to-visit-the-reichstag-dome-for-free-and-why-you-should-book-now).

**My advice: do not try to enter everything.** The mistake I see most is people treating this as a checklist and buying tickets for five attractions they will rush through. Walk the line from Alexanderplatz to the Gate first, free, and see what you actually want to go back into. Most of the value of central Berlin is outside, on the street, not behind a ticket barrier.

## How far is it really

The whole route from Alexanderplatz to the Brandenburg Gate is roughly **2 to 2.5 kilometres**, which is 30 to 40 minutes of steady walking, or a slow two to three hours if you stop and look at things, which you should. It is flat the entire way. There are benches, cafés and public toilets along it, and shade in summer near the river and the Lustgarten.

If you are wondering whether Berlin as a whole is this walkable, the short answer is that the centre is and the outer city is not, which I cover in [is Berlin walkable](https://www.berlinwalk.com/post/is-berlin-walkable). For the official transport picture if you do want to hop a train, [Berlin.de](https://www.berlin.de/en/public-transportation/) has the current network and fares.
