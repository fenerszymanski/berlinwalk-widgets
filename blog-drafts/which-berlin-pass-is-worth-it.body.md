Every first-time visitor hits the same small wall at BER Airport or the first U-Bahn station: there is a rack of Berlin passes, each promising to save you money, and no obvious way to know which one is actually for you. WelcomeCard, CityTourCard, Museum Pass Berlin, plus the plain single ticket sitting quietly next to them. They are not the same product, and buying the wrong one is one of the easier ways to waste 30 euros on your first morning.

My advice is to stop asking which pass is "best" and start asking which one fits the trip you are actually going to have. A three-day museum marathon and a two-day walking-and-eating weekend want completely different tickets. Once you know the shape of your days, the right card is usually obvious.

## Which Berlin pass is worth it: the honest short answer

There is no single winner, and any guide that gives you one is selling something. The Berlin passes split cleanly by what they cover:

- **Transport cards** (WelcomeCard, CityTourCard): unlimited public transport for a set number of days, plus partner discounts. They do **not** give free museum entry on their own.
- **Museum Pass Berlin**: free entry to 30+ museums over three days. It gives you **no transport at all**.
- **Single and day tickets**: the plain BVG fare. No discounts, no frills, but often the cheapest option for a short, central, walkable trip.

So the real question is a two-part one: how much will you ride, and how many paid museums will you actually enter? Answer those honestly and the decision makes itself. The tool below does the arithmetic for your specific trip.

## First, the option most guides skip: no pass at all

Before you buy anything, look at the plain fares, because Berlin is more walkable than most visitors expect and my own [free walking tour](https://www.berlinwalk.com) route stays inside the normal AB city zone the whole way.

In 2026 the [current BVG fares](https://www.berlin.de/en/public-transportation/1772016-2913840-tickets-fares-and-route-maps.en.html) are:

- **Single ticket AB: €4.00** (a short hop of up to three stops is €2.80).
- **24-hour day ticket AB: €11.20** — unlimited travel for one person for a full day.
- **4-trip ticket AB: €12.40** (€3.10 a trip) if you ride only now and then.

That day-ticket number is the one to remember, because it is the benchmark every transport card is measured against. A 72-hour CityTourCard costs €35.60, which is €11.87 a day — almost exactly the price of buying a day ticket each morning. In other words, the transport cards are not magically cheaper than paying as you go. What you are really buying with them is the bundle of partner discounts and the convenience of not thinking about tickets. If you are staying central and walking most of the time, three separate day tickets, or even a handful of single fares, can come out cheaper than any card. For how the zones work and when you need the ABC ticket for the airport or Potsdam, see my guide to [Berlin's AB and ABC ticket zones](https://www.berlinwalk.com/post/berlin-ab-abc-ticket-zones).

## The transport cards: WelcomeCard vs CityTourCard

These two are close cousins. Both give unlimited AB (or ABC) transport for your chosen number of days and a folder of discounts. The differences are small but real.

**Berlin WelcomeCard** starts at **€28.50 for 48 hours** and runs to around **€34 for 72 hours** in zone AB (check the current price, as it drifts a little each year). Its selling point is the discount network: up to 50% off at 170-plus partners, which is the widest of any Berlin card. If you are the kind of traveller who will actually use discounts on a river cruise, the TV Tower, or a museum or two, the WelcomeCard usually earns its keep. I broke the maths down in detail in [Is the Berlin WelcomeCard worth it?](https://www.berlinwalk.com/post/is-the-berlin-welcomecard-worth-it-in-2026-an-honest-breakdown).

**Berlin CityTourCard** costs **€35.60 for 72 hours** in zone AB. Its discounts are shallower (up to about 30% at roughly 30 partners), so it competes mainly on transport plus a smaller set of deals. It can undercut the WelcomeCard on the longer durations, so if you are staying five or six days and do not care about the discount breadth, price it against the WelcomeCard for your exact number of days.

My honest read: for a normal two-to-three-day Berlin trip, the transport card only wins clearly if you are riding a lot **and** will use the discounts. If you are walking a compact central route and riding a few times a day, a day ticket when you need one is simpler and often cheaper. If you are unsure how much you will really ride, read [is Berlin walkable?](https://www.berlinwalk.com/post/is-berlin-walkable) first — the answer changes a lot of pass decisions.

## The museum question: where Museum Pass Berlin wins

If your Berlin is built around exhibitions, the picture flips completely. The **Museum Pass Berlin costs €32** and gives free entry to [more than 30 museums over three consecutive days](https://www.visitberlin.de/en/museum-pass-berlin), including all five Museum Island houses, the Jewish Museum, the German Museum of Technology, and the Natural History Museum.

To see why that is strong, do the single-ticket maths. A Museum Island house is roughly €12 to €19 on its own. Enter three museums and the €32 pass has already paid for itself; a serious museum trip visiting five or six easily doubles its value. That is a better museum deal than either transport card offers, and I compared it directly against buying tickets one by one in [Museum Pass vs single tickets](https://www.berlinwalk.com/post/berlin-museum-pass-vs-single-tickets-which-one-saves-you-money).

![The restored grand staircase inside the Neues Museum on Berlin's Museum Island](blog-drafts/images/which-berlin-pass-is-worth-it/assets/optimized/05-neues-museum-staircase.jpg)
_The Neues Museum is one of five Museum Island houses the Museum Pass covers. Enter three of them and the €32 pass has already paid for itself._

The catch is right there in the fine print: **the Museum Pass includes no transport whatsoever.** You will still need day tickets, single fares, or your own two feet to get between the museums. That is fine — most of Museum Island is a short walk from Alexanderplatz and Hackescher Markt — but it means the Museum Pass is a companion to a transport plan, not a replacement for one.

There is also a hybrid worth knowing about: the **WelcomeCard Museum Island** version, around **€62 for 72 hours**, bundles AB transport with one free daily entry to the Museum Island group. If your trip is Museum Island plus a lot of riding, price that single card against the alternative of a Museum Pass (€32) plus day tickets and decide which suits your days.

## Match the pass to your trip

Here is how I actually steer visitors, once I know what their days look like:

- **Short, central, walking trip, one or two museums.** Skip the passes. Buy single tickets when you ride and a €11.20 day ticket on any heavy-transport day. Berlin rewards walking, and you will likely spend less than any card.
- **Transport-heavy, few museums** (day trips inside the city, lots of hopping between districts). A transport card can pay off, mainly for the discounts. Take the WelcomeCard if you will use its wider partner network; take the CityTourCard if it is cheaper for your exact number of days and you do not care about deals.
- **Museum-heavy, light transport.** The Museum Pass Berlin at €32 is the clear value pick. Pair it with a day ticket or two, or just walk between the central museums.
- **Both museum-heavy and transport-heavy.** Compare the WelcomeCard Museum Island (€62) against a Museum Pass (€32) plus the day tickets you would buy anyway. For three focused days this is usually the closest call, and the tool above is the fastest way to settle it.

One more thing that is not a tourist pass at all: the **Deutschlandticket** at €63 a month. It is a subscription, not a holiday ticket, and it only makes sense for long or very transport-heavy stays. I explain exactly when it is and is not worth it in [the Deutschlandticket guide for tourists](https://www.berlinwalk.com/post/deutschlandticket-berlin-tourists).

## A quick worked example

Say you are here for three days, staying near Hackescher Markt, and you plan to visit three museums and ride the U-Bahn a few times a day. Pay-as-you-go with a day ticket each day is about €33.60 in transport, plus roughly €40 in museum entries if you buy them singly — call it €73. Swap the single museum tickets for a €32 Museum Pass and you are at about €65.60, and you have unlocked 30-plus museums instead of three. A WelcomeCard Museum Island at €62 covers transport and the Museum Island houses in one card, but only the Museum Island group, not the wider 30-plus. For that specific trip, Museum Pass plus day tickets and the WelcomeCard Museum Island land within a few euros of each other, and the deciding factor is simply which museums you want. That is the whole point: the "best" Berlin pass is the one that matches the museums and rides you were going to do anyway.
